Chapter 1 · Before Brexit

A different starting point

Before January 2021, EU citizens could work in the UK without needing a visa at all. Employer demand for sponsorship was low, and mostly limited to specialist roles from outside the EU.

UK vacancies across these five sectors sat around 264,000 at the start of 2021 — the baseline this whole story moves away from.

Chapter 2 · Brexit and COVID

Two shocks, almost at once

Brexit ended free movement for EU citizens in January 2021. Employers who had previously hired EU workers without a visa now had to sponsor them under the same route used for international students.

At nearly the same time, the UK was still working through the disruption of the COVID-19 pandemic across the whole economy.

Jan 2021 — free movement ends
Chapter 3 · The 2022 vacancy boom

Everyone was hiring at once

As the economy reopened, vacancies rose sharply, and employers who had previously relied on EU labour turned to the Skilled Worker sponsorship route for the first time — many of them for the first time ever.

By 2022, the pattern that would shape the rest of this story was already forming.

2022 — vacancies climbing fast
Chapter 4 · The threshold rises

The rules changed twice

The government raised the minimum salary threshold for Skilled Worker sponsorship in April 2024, then again in April 2025. Roles that were sponsorable a few years ago may not be anymore.

By 2024, Education's median salary had dropped below the new threshold. Healthcare joined it in 2025.

£26,200 → £41,700 in four years
Chapter 5 · Not every sector felt it the same way

Diverging paths

Healthcare visa grants nearly quintupled between 2021 and 2023, driven largely by care worker roles being added to the sponsorship list — then fell even more sharply after the 2024 and 2025 threshold rises, down to under a tenth of their 2023 peak.

Technology followed a smaller version of the same rise, then a steadier decline. Your sector's shape matters as much as the headline trend.
